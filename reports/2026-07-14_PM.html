🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 68/100 — Sticky, not accelerating — but stuck meaningfully above target and with the wrong internals. The core-trend measures cluster around 2.6-2.8% (Median CPI 2.71%, 16% trimmed-mean CPI 2.63%, Median PCE 2.82%, core sticky CPI 2.81%) and every one of them sits in the 56th-71st percentile of its own history with z-scores hovering near zero — this is inflation that has stopped falling rather than inflation that is re-igniting. The problem is the composition. Flexible CPI is running at 5.06% and rising (76th percentile, z-score 0.56), which is the energy and goods channel lighting up exactly as Brent breaks $85 on the Hormuz escalation; trimmed-mean PCE is also flagged rising. Meanwhile the Dallas Fed distribution shows 24% of the PCE basket rising faster than 5% and a further 34% in the 3-5% band — a majority of the basket is above target — even though 27.2% is outright falling. Headline Core PCE at 3.41% is the number that should worry the Fed, and it sits well above Core CPI at 2.6%. Set against that, the market prices only 2.29% over five years and 1.10% over one year, and wage growth (3.5% average hourly earnings, ECI at just 0.9%) is not validating a wage-price spiral. Bottom line: the disinflation is over, the last mile is not being walked, and the oil shock is arriving on top of a basket that never fully cooled. This is the setup that makes a hawkish Warsh Fed credible.

Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The honest synthesis: this is not 2000, and pretending otherwise has cost people three years of returns. But it is not innocent either, and the danger is in a different place than last time. In 2000 the froth was in price multiples on companies with no earnings. Today the froth is in *credit* — spreads tighter than the dot-com peak in the computed data, financial conditions looser, VIX lower — and in *concentration*, where the top ten names carry a share of the index that has no precedent. The 2000 bubble deflated because a Fed hiking into an inverted curve killed the funding; the curve today is positively sloped and the ISMs are expanding, so the trigger is absent. What is present is a market that has crowded into equities versus every real asset (the commodities/equities ratio at 200% of the 2000 condition), levered up (margin debt +40.7% YoY), and priced credit for zero accidents.
