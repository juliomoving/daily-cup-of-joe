Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The 2000 parallel is real but uneven. What rhymes: extreme index concentration (top-10 ~35.6% of the S&P, analyst estimate), an all-time-high Buffett ratio (233.8%), a CAPE (40.4) within a few points of the dot-com record, semis trading at a richer multiple than the 2000 peak, and complacency markers (low VIX before today, loose financial conditions, tight credit). What does NOT rhyme: the broad Nasdaq multiple is a fraction of 2000's, price-stretch vs trend is modest, IPO froth (~42/100, analyst estimate) is elevated but far from the 2000 deluge, and elevated long-term EPS-growth expectations (~70/100, analyst estimate) are at least anchored to real AI cash flows rather than eyeball metrics. Today's VIX spike to 20.15 is the kind of tremor that punctuates late-cycle melt-ups. The risk is narrowness: with a third of the index in ten names and semis priced for perfection, a single leadership stumble (e.g., a soft MU print) can drive index-level drawdowns disproportionate to the breadth of bad news.

🏦Fed Chair Warsh
NEAR TERM

Likely HOLD at the next meeting with a hawkish bias. Warsh (sworn in May 22, 2026) chaired his first FOMC June 16-17 and left the policy rate unchanged, underscoring determination to return inflation to the 2% target. Given still-sticky core inflation (Core PCE 3.29%, sticky CPI 3.12% rising) and his on-record skepticism of pre-committing, expect no cut and resistance to signaling one — inferred from his first-meeting stance and prior writings.

~1 YEAR

Fewer cuts than the market hopes and a higher bar to ease. Over ~12 months expect a data-dependent, inflation-first stance: cuts only if the labor market (unemployment 4.3%) deteriorates materially or core inflation convincingly breaks toward 2%. Warsh has historically favored a smaller Fed footprint and rules-based credibility, so a structurally more hawkish reaction function than the late-Powell Fed is the base case.
