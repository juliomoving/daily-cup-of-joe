⚔️Iran War

The conflict is in a fragile, contested ceasefire. A US–Iran memorandum signed June 17 by Trump and President Pezeshkian set a 60-day window for a nuclear agreement, included a cessation of hostilities in Lebanon, an end to Iranian restrictions on the Strait of Hormuz, a drawdown of US assets and sanctions relief. That framework frayed over the weekend: after consecutive nights of US strikes and Iranian strikes on US bases in two Gulf states, both sides agreed a temporary stand-down on June 29. Trump warned Iran 'will no longer exist' if strikes resume and announced technical talks in Doha on June 30 — but senior Iranian negotiator Kazem Gharibabadi said those reports were 'not confirmed.' Iran continues to press a disputed demand for Hormuz transit tolls and struck a vessel in the strait, pushing some ships onto a route hugging the Omani coast. Net market read: a live geopolitical tail (oil/VIX) that is currently de-escalating but unresolved.

Two-sided and data-dependent. Warsh's stated agenda — lower rates 'over time,' a smaller balance sheet, and a rethink of how the Fed measures inflation (five new task forces) — plus his view that the AI boom is 'structurally disinflationary' biases him toward eventual easing. But with core PCE at 3.41% and broad-based price pressure, a hike-or-hold path likely precedes any cuts. Net one-year: roughly flat to modestly lower policy rate, contingent on a clear disinflation trend; meaningful easing only if AI-productivity disinflation shows up in the data. (Inferred — flagged where not explicitly stated.)

The regime shift is procedural as much as directional: Warsh scrapped forward guidance ('not well-suited to the current policy conjuncture'), a sharp break from the Powell era, and is restructuring how the Fed communicates and measures inflation.
