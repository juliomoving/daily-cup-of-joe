Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The 2000 analogy is strongest exactly where it should worry you — you are paying near-record CAPE (40.4 vs a ~44 dot-com peak) and a record-adjacent ~2.3x-GDP Buffett indicator to own a market where ~35% of the S&P sits in ten mostly-AI names, and where semis (SOX 57.6x) are the runaway leadership just as tech/telecom were in 1999–2000. But the classic top requires the crowd to be all-in and credit to crack, and neither is true: AAII is net-bearish (-10.9), consumer confidence is subdued (91.2), the yield curve has un-inverted rather than deepened, and PMIs are still expansionary. The tell to watch is the Nasdaq itself slipping below its 50DMA (computed -0.31%) with a 60% death-cross reversal probability — froth concentrated in a narrowing leadership is how these episodes roll over before the breadth data confirms it. Verdict: late-cycle and richly valued, dangerous concentration, but not the blow-off euphoria of March 2000 — yet.
