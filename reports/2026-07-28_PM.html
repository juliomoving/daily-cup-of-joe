The tape today was a rotation, not a risk-off event, and the dispersion underneath the indices was the real story. The second story was crude. WTI fell 4.06% to 79.26 and Brent dropped 4.50% (-3.98) to 84.38, unwinding war premium after the US paused new strikes on Iran and Tehran said it is not currently negotiating. Sentiment reads constructively-defensive rather than euphoric. Health Care (+2.33%), Consumer Staples (+1.96%), Materials (+1.66%) and Comm Services (+1.64%) led; Energy (-1.40%) and Info Tech (-1.17%) lagged. Credit was the one place that leaned slightly the other way — every OAS we track was flat-to-wider, with EUR HY +5bp to 2.90% the largest move. Versus the prior session, the message is that investors are paying up for cash-flow certainty and shedding the crowded semis/AI trade, and they are doing it without demanding a volatility premium. That is a comfortable configuration right up until it isn't — which is precisely why tomorrow's FOMC and Thursday's Apple print matter more than the index changes suggest.

🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 71/100 — Sticky, broad, and not yet beaten — with a genuine near-term downdraft from energy that will flatter the headline without fixing the core. The honest read is that the underlying-inflation measures cluster in a tight 2.4%-2.85% band (Median CPI 2.71455%, 16% Trimmed-Mean CPI 2.62829%, Median PCE 2.81557%, Trimmed-Mean PCE 2.41%, Sticky CPI 2.8427%, Core Sticky CPI 2.8069%) — every one of them above the 2% target, none of them accelerating dramatically, and all of them sitting in the upper half of their own historical distributions per the computed core-trend percentile work. Core PCE YoY at 3.41204% is the outlier that should worry policymakers most, running a full 80 basis points above the median-based gauges, which tells you the tails of the distribution — not the middle — are doing the damage. Core CPI at 2.6% is the friendliest number in the panel and is the one that will be quoted by doves. The breadth data is the part that decides the verdict. The Dallas Fed distribution shows 14.0% of the PCE basket rising faster than 10%, another 10.0% in the 5-10% bucket, and 34.0% in the 3-5% bucket — so 58% of the basket by weight is inflating at 3% or faster, against just 9.6% in the 0-2% zone, 5.2% in the 2-3% zone, and 27.2% outright falling. An inflation problem that were genuinely resolving would show mass migrating into the 0-3% buckets; instead the modal component is running 3-5%. That is not a relative-price story, it is a general-price-level story, and it is why I put persistence at 71 out of 100 rather than somewhere in the fifties. The offsetting evidence is real and should not be dismissed. Sticky CPI (2.8427%) and Core Sticky CPI (2.8069%) are both falling on the computed direction flags — the slow-moving, expectations-laden component is decelerating. Flexible CPI at 5.0616% is rising and is the most stretched measure in the set, but flexible prices are exactly what today's 4.5% Brent decline and 3.79% nat gas decline will attack over the next two prints. Market-implied expectations are well behaved: the 5-year breakeven at 2.1781% is barely above target and the 1-year at 1.7539% is BELOW it, meaning the bond market is pricing a genuine near-term disinflation — almost certainly the energy channel. Wages corroborate the benign case: Average Hourly Earnings at 3.5% and, strikingly, ECI at 0.9% are not consistent with a wage-price spiral. So: the war-driven energy spike is unwinding in real time and will pull headline inflation down over the next two to three months. Underneath it, a majority of the consumption basket is still inflating above 3%, core PCE has a 3-handle, and the median-based measures have flatlined rather than fallen. That is the profile of an inflation that stalls in the high 2s rather than one that returns to target — which is precisely the problem Warsh has inherited and precisely why his committee is discussing a hike rather than a cut.
