🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 62/100 — Sticky, not accelerating — and the disinflation that remains is arriving through goods and energy rather than through the services core, which is exactly the composition that keeps a central bank from cutting quickly. The core-trend measures cluster tightly in the mid-2s: Median CPI 2.71%, 16% trimmed-mean CPI 2.63%, Median PCE 2.66%, trimmed-mean PCE 2.23%, sticky CPI 2.84% and core sticky CPI 2.81%. All six sit above target and none of them is moving fast. Crucially, they are not falling as a group — the computed directions show Median CPI and trimmed-mean CPI flat, with Median PCE, trimmed-mean PCE and core sticky CPI falling; nothing in the underlying core is rising. The exception is flexible CPI at 5.06%, which is both the highest reading and the only one tagged as rising, and it carries the highest percentile of the group. Flexible prices are the volatile, energy-and-goods-sensitive component, so today's 3.73% drop in WTI and 3.06% drop in natural gas should mechanically pull that number down over coming months — the market is already discounting it, with the 1-year breakeven at just 1.6568% versus the 5-year at 2.2407%. That inversion is the single most informative number in the panel: markets expect the next twelve months to be materially cooler than the following four years. Breadth is the argument against complacency. On the Dallas Fed distribution, 28.1% of the PCE basket is outright falling and 24.3% is running between 0 and 2% — so 52.4% of the basket is at or below target, which is genuine progress. But 23.5% is still running above 5%, including 8.1% above 10%, and another 5.8% in the 3-5% band. A basket where roughly a quarter of components run above 5% is not a basket that has normalised; it is a bimodal distribution being averaged into a comfortable-looking headline. The wage and demand side is cooling in a way that supports the disinflationary case: average hourly earnings 3.5%, ECI 0.9%, JOLTS openings 7,594k, quits 2.0, payrolls 57k and unemployment 4.2%. That is a labour market losing momentum without breaking — initial claims at 197k are still historically low. Core PCE at 3.28653% remains the awkward headline outlier, well above every trimmed measure, which usually means a small number of large components are doing the work. My read: inflation settles in the 2.5-2.8% range rather than returning to 2%, energy provides a visible but temporary downdraft over the next two quarters, and the services core proves the binding constraint. That argues for a Fed that holds longer than the equity market currently assumes.

Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The most useful way to hold this is that today's market is a valuation bubble without a behavioural bubble. The current cycle indexed from October 2022 sits at 235.8, having run from 100 over 46 months. Anchored at the 1994 pre-internet reset, the analogue was near 265 at month 46 and peaked at month 69 near 665. On both anchors, the current run is slower and shallower than the dot-com path at the equivalent point.
