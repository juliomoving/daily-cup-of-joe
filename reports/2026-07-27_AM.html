🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 68/100 — Sticky, broad, and unhelpfully high — and today's oil collapse will make it look better than it is. Core PCE at 3.41% year over year is the number that matters and it is well above target, while headline-friendly Core CPI at 2.60% flatters the picture. The underlying-inflation panel is what settles the argument: Median CPI 2.71%, 16% trimmed-mean CPI 2.63%, Median PCE 2.82%, trimmed-mean PCE 2.41%, sticky CPI 2.84% and core sticky CPI 2.81% — a tight cluster in the high-2s that has barely moved. The computed core-trend work has these measures sitting between the 56th and 71st percentile of their own histories with z-scores from -0.12 to +0.22, with trimmed-mean PCE rising and only the sticky measures falling. That is not disinflation; that is a plateau. Breadth is the real indictment: on the Dallas Fed distribution, 14.0% of the PCE basket is rising faster than 10%, another 10.0% is in the 5-10% bucket and 34.0% is in the 3-5% bucket — 58% of the basket is inflating above 3% — while only 9.6% sits in the 0-2% comfort zone and 27.2% is outright falling. The market disagrees with all of this: the 5-year breakeven is 2.2262% and the 1-year is just 1.8209%, meaning investors are pricing a return to target that the underlying data does not support. Flexible CPI at 5.06% and rising is where today's oil move will bite hardest and fastest — expect the headline to improve markedly over the next two prints while the core barely budges. The honest read: inflation is not accelerating, but it has stopped falling at a level roughly a full point above where the Fed wants it, with wages at 3.5% providing a floor. That is exactly the configuration that keeps Warsh's committee talking about hikes.
