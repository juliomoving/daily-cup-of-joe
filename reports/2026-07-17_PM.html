🧮Fixed Income Spreadscomputed

The bond market is refusing to panic. US investment-grade spreads — the extra yield companies pay over Treasuries — sit at 0.76% (LUACOAS), a 2nd-percentile, z-score -0.85 reading that is near the tightest in history and miles from any crisis marker (0% of the way to 2008/2011/2020/2022 stress). US high-yield at 2.66% (z -0.91, 4th percentile) tells the same story. European IG/HY nudged wider by a basis point or three but remain historically compressed; EM USD OAS at 1.65 is 4th-percentile tight. Translation: lenders are demanding almost no extra compensation for risk — pricing in near-zero default stress even as oil spikes and equities wobble. That is complacency, not confirmation of safety. Key levels to watch: US HY would need to widen toward ~4% (from 2.66%) and IG toward ~1.0% before signaling genuine credit stress; the conditions to get there would be a sustained oil-into-inflation spiral forcing real Fed hikes, or a growth scare that cracks earnings — neither is priced today.

⚔️Iran War

The conflict is escalating, not cooling. The interim US-Iran ceasefire collapsed on July 8, and US forces have now conducted strikes for a sixth consecutive night as of July 16, including the campaign's first daylight raids and inland strikes in Sistan and Baluchestan on July 15.
