Every one of these sits at 0% of the widening seen in the GFC 2008, Euro-debt 2011, COVID 2020 and 2022-hiking episodes, which is the computed way of saying no crisis is being discounted at all. Today's moves — IG +1bp, HY +4bp, EUR IG +2bp, EUR HY +3bp, EM flat — are noise, not a turn. Key levels to trade against. On US IG, 0.71% is the historical floor; a print there would mark a genuine blow-off in credit and, historically, has been a better sell signal for risk than any equity indicator. On the widening side, IG at 1.00% is the first level that would force real mark-to-market pain in long-duration corporate books, and 1.22% is the level the computed bubble panel benchmarks against the 2000 peak. On US HY, 2.33% is the record tight and only 32bp below spot; 3.50% is the first level that would signal an actual credit cycle turning, and 4.50% would confirm it. What would get us there? Three conditions, in order of likelihood: (1) a genuine energy shock — Brent sustained above roughly $110 on a Hormuz closure, which raises input costs and squeezes the low-rated issuers hardest; (2) confirmation that the labour market is contracting rather than cooling — the last payroll print was outright negative at -23k, and a second negative print with initial claims breaking above roughly 250k from today's 203k would reprice HY quickly; (3) an AI-capex disappointment that forces the market to reconsider the debt-funded portion of the datacentre build, which is precisely why tonight's Broadcom print matters more to credit than to equities. Absent one of those, spreads have no reason to move, and that is the problem: an asset class that only widens on a shock is an asset class with no cushion.
