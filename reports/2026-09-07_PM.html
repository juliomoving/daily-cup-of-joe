IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The most useful way to read the chart panels is that the current cycle, anchored at the October-2022 bear-market low, is 47 months in and sits at 241.2 on a rebased index. The 1990-anchored dot-com path was at roughly 235-243 around months 38-39 and did not peak until month 113 at 1,423.9; the 1994-anchored path was near 238.8 at month 39 and peaked at month 69 at 665.3. So on trajectory alone the current run is neither unusually fast nor obviously late — it is tracking the *early-to-middle* portion of the dot-com ascent, and the valuation marker sits at just 21% of the peak condition. That is genuinely reassuring on the price question. What is not reassuring is that this run has been achieved with volatility and credit priced as though the destination were already known. In 2000, the market at least demanded some compensation on the way up — the reference VIX was 23.1. Today it is 15.30, and the model reads that as twice as complacent as the actual dot-com peak. The same is true of credit: IG at 0.80% (133% of peak condition) and Baa-10Y at 156bp (155%) mean corporate borrowers are being charged less for risk today than they were at the top of the greatest equity mania in modern history. The commodities-vs-equities ratio at 0.02, or 200% of the peak condition, closes the argument — capital has abandoned real assets for financial ones more completely than in 1999. Overlay my estimates on that and the shape is clear. A CAPE near 40.5x is 92% of the 2000 top. Market-cap-to-GDP near 225% is well *above* it. Top-10 concentration around 41.5% is far above it. Long-term EPS growth expectations near 72 out of 100 are in signpost territory. The mania is not in the multiple applied to the average stock; it is in how much of the market is one bet, how large that market is relative to the economy it sits on, and how little anyone is being paid to hedge it. The semis are the pressure point — a 53.42x SOX P/E at 84% of peak-2000 conditions is where the concentration, the AI capital-expenditure story and the earnings-expectation stretch all intersect. Which makes Oracle's Thursday-night print, and the cloud-growth guidance inside it, the single most consequential scheduled event of the week for this entire thesis. The honest verdict: this is not 2000, and forecasting a -78% analogue would be intellectually lazy. But the specific things that made 2000's aftermath so destructive — extreme concentration, borrowed money, and an absence of anyone on the other side of the trade — are more present today than the headline P/E suggests, and the cheapest insurance in the world right now is volatility and credit protection.
