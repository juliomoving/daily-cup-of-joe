🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 62/100 — Inflation is not accelerating, but it is not going away either — and the composition has quietly turned adversarial. The headline tension is that core PCE is running at 3.29% year-over-year while core CPI is only 2.50%; that gap is unusually wide and it is the PCE measure the Fed targets. The underlying-inflation panel splits the same way. The persistent, slow-moving measures are all clustered in a 2.2% to 2.9% band and mostly falling: median CPI 2.71%, 16% trimmed-mean CPI 2.63%, median PCE 2.66%, trimmed-mean PCE 2.23%, sticky CPI 2.84%, core sticky CPI 2.81%. Every one of those measures sits in the 56th-to-70th percentile of its own history — above normal, not extreme, and drifting down. But flexible CPI is 5.06%, in the 77th percentile with a z-score of 0.56, and it is the single measure in the panel that is RISING. Flexible prices are the ones that respond to commodities, and gold +1.33%, silver +2.52% and copper +0.84% overnight tell you exactly which way that component is heading next. On breadth, the Dallas Fed distribution is where the real answer lives: 8.1% of the PCE basket is inflating faster than 10%, another 15.4% is running 5-10%, and 5.8% is in the 3-5% band — so 29.3% of the basket, roughly a third, is rising faster than the Fed's comfort zone. Against that, 28.1% of the basket is outright falling and 24.3% is running under 2%. That is a genuinely bimodal distribution: a large disinflating core and a stubborn, commodity-sensitive tail that will not close. The computed Core-Trend Sub-Score confirms the persistent measures are running above their historical norms rather than at them. Wage pressure is no longer the problem — average hourly earnings 3.2% and the employment cost index at 0.9% are consistent with 2% inflation, and with payrolls at -23k the labour channel is closing fast. Market expectations agree: the 5-year breakeven is 2.25% and the 1-year is only 1.75%, so investors are pricing near-term disinflation. The verdict: persistence is moderate and slowly improving in the wage-and-services core, but the goods-and-commodity tail is re-accelerating and the breadth is too wide to declare victory. Getting core PCE from 3.29% to 2% requires the flexible component to stop rising — and the metals tape overnight says it is not going to. Call it sticky in the high 2s through year-end unless the labour contraction deepens sharply.
