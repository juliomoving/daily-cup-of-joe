The crisis comparison is the punchline: against the GFC 2008, Euro debt crisis 2011, COVID 2020 and the 2022 hiking cycle, every single index registers 0 — today's spreads are zero percent of the way toward any of those four stress regimes. Key levels. US IG's historical minimum is 71bp; at 78bp there are seven basis points of tightening left in all of recorded history, and 618bp of widening room to the maximum. Practically: the first meaningful pain threshold for HY is 300bp (a 34bp move that would erase the year's carry for most levered credit books), the level at which allocators typically start de-risking is 350-400bp, and 500bp is where primary issuance windows historically shut. For IG, 100bp is the psychological line and 125bp is where the bid for duration-heavy paper thins out. The conditions to get there: (1) a sustained Brent move above roughly $110 from a durable Hormuz closure, which converts an energy headline into a margin shock for European and Asian industrials and an inflation shock the Fed cannot look through; (2) two more negative payroll prints on top of the current -23k, which would move the discussion from soft landing to earnings recession; (3) a Warsh hike, which given a Fed that has already removed its easing bias would be a genuine surprise to a market carrying record-percentile-tight spreads; or (4) an EM funding accident, where a 3rd-percentile EM USD spread and an oil-import shock meet in a country with thin reserves. Absent one of those, spreads can grind sideways for a long time — but at the 4th percentile you are being paid almost nothing to find out. The asymmetry is as poor as it has ever been.
