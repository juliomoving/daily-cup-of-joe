European credit is only marginally less rich — EUR IG 82bp (10th percentile, z -0.83), EUR HY 286bp (10th percentile, z -0.71). The computed crisis comparisons read 0% of the GFC 2008, EuroDebt 2011, COVID 2020 and Hike 2022 stress levels across every index. In plain terms: spreads carry essentially no compensation for the war premium the oil market is charging. Friday's tape was consistent with that — HY tightened 7bp to 265bp while European spreads leaked slightly wider (EUR IG +1bp to 82, EUR HY +5bp to 286), a small transatlantic divergence worth watching. Levels and the conditions to reach them: US HY needs only 32bp of tightening to set an all-time record (233bp) and it would take a genuine recession scare to get to 400bp — call that a Fed hike plus a quarter of negative payrolls. IG at 100bp, a level that would barely register historically, requires 22bp of widening and would most plausibly come from a hawkish Wednesday plus $120 Brent squeezing margins. The asymmetry is the whole point: at the 3rd percentile you are being paid almost nothing to be long credit risk and you own most of the downside. If Brent holds above $110 into month-end and the Fed hikes, HY at 300-320bp is a reasonable first stop; below 250bp is the squeeze scenario if the Fed holds and oil mean-reverts.
