Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

Valuation marker: today's Nasdaq P/E (40x) ≈ 23% of the ~175x 2000-peak P/E.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The bubble debate is really a concentration debate. The computed indicators say the vulnerabilities are (1) semiconductor valuations at near-peak conditions, (2) credit complacency at cycle tights, and (3) volatility/positioning complacency with no hedges bid — a combination that is dangerous not because it must break, but because there is zero margin of safety if a catalyst (Iran/oil, a growth scare, an AI capex disappointment) arrives. My estimates (CAPE 41.6, Buffett 232%, all analyst estimates) confirm the aggregate market is expensive by every long-horizon yardstick, yet the absence of blow-off breadth (broad Nasdaq P/E only ~40x, not triple-digit) and the narrower leadership argue this is a stretched late-cycle regime, not the terminal 2000 mania. Watch the semis and IG spreads — those are where the reset, if it comes, starts.
