🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 63/100 — Sticky, not spiralling — and no longer improving. The honest read of this panel is that US inflation has stopped falling somewhere in the high-2s to low-3s and is now grinding sideways with an upward tail risk from energy. Headline core measures disagree with each other in a revealing way: Core PCE YoY sits at 3.28653% while Core CPI YoY is only 2.6% — a gap that wide usually means the composition, not the trend, is doing the work. The underlying-inflation family clusters tightly and unhelpfully close to target-plus: Median CPI 2.71455%, 16% Trimmed-Mean CPI 2.62829%, Median PCE 2.66027%, Trimmed-Mean PCE 2.23%, Sticky CPI 2.8427% and Core Sticky CPI 2.8069%. Every one of those is above 2%, and the computed core-trend work shows the median, trimmed-mean and sticky measures all sitting in the 56th-to-70th percentile of their own histories with z-scores between -0.12 and +0.09 — statistically normal, directionally flat-to-slightly-falling, and stubbornly above where the Fed wants them. The outlier is the one that matters most right now: Flexible CPI at 5.0616%, in the 77th percentile with a z-score of +0.56 and explicitly rising. Flexible prices are the energy- and commodity-sensitive components, and with a shooting conflict around the Strait of Hormuz and Brent at $79.70, that is the transmission channel to watch — flexible inflation running at nearly double the sticky rate is exactly how a supply shock starts leaking into expectations. Breadth is the second problem. The Dallas Fed distribution shows 8.1% of the PCE basket rising faster than 10% and another 15.4% rising 5-10% — so roughly a quarter of consumer spending is experiencing inflation at 5%+, against 24.3% in the 0-2% comfort zone and 28.1% outright falling. A market with 28.1% of the basket in deflation and 23.5% above 5% is not a market converging on 2%; it is two economies averaging to a number nobody experiences. The offsetting good news is in expectations and wages: the 5-year breakeven is 2.1904% and the 1-year is 1.5985% — markets simply do not believe an inflation problem is coming — while Average Hourly Earnings at 3.5% and ECI at 0.9% show no wage-price spiral. That combination is what keeps this a persistence story rather than an acceleration story. Net: a Fed with a hiking bias under a new chair has enough evidence to stay on hold and enough evidence to hike if flexible prices keep climbing; it has almost none to cut. Wednesday's CPI is the swing factor.

~1 YEAR
