🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 68/100 — Sticky, and stickier than the headline suggests — but the composition just changed in a way that should lower the persistence read over the next two months. Start with the level divergence, which is the first thing to explain: Core PCE at 3.41% versus Core CPI at 2.60% is an 81bp gap, and the Fed targets the higher one. The core-trend family clusters in a tight band well above target — Median CPI 2.71%, 16% trimmed-mean CPI 2.63%, Median PCE 2.82%, trimmed-mean PCE 2.41%, core sticky CPI 2.81% — and every one of those measures sits in the upper half of its own history, with the trimmed-mean PCE and flexible components still rising while the sticky components are falling. That directional split is the whole story. Sticky CPI at 2.84% falling and core sticky at 2.81% falling means the services/wage-embedded component is genuinely cooling — corroborated by average hourly earnings at 3.5% and an ECI at 0.9%, neither of which is consistent with a wage-price spiral. Meanwhile flexible CPI at 5.06% and RISING is doing the damage, and flexible CPI is overwhelmingly energy, food and goods. Which is precisely what a 149-day oil war produces — and precisely what a 5.14% one-day collapse in WTI starts to unwind. Now the breadth, which is where I part company with the sanguine read. The Dallas Fed distribution shows 14.0% of the PCE basket rising faster than 10%, another 10.0% in the 5–10% band and 34.0% in the 3–5% band — that is 58% of the basket running above 3%, against only 9.6% in the 0–2% comfort zone and 5.2% in the 2–3% band. Just 27.2% is outright falling. Inflation this broad does not return to 2% quickly, because there is no narrow set of categories you can point to and wait out. Against that, the market flatly disagrees with me: the 5y breakeven is 2.2578% and the 1y breakeven is just 1.919% — investors are pricing near-target inflation within a year and are willing to fund that view. The reconciliation is the oil break. If crude holds near 84.72, the flexible component rolls over mechanically, headline follows, and the breakevens are vindicated. If the Iran pause fails and Brent retakes 100, the 58%-above-3% breadth becomes the base for a genuine second wave, and a Warsh Fed that has already dropped forward guidance will not be slow about it. My persistence read stays in the high-60s: too broad to fade fast, but with the marginal driver now moving the right way.

~1 YEAR

Higher for longer, with the balance of risk tilted toward one or two hikes rather than cuts, and a materially smaller balance sheet. Reported strategist consensus is for rates to stay steady through the remainder of 2026 as rising prices and volatile energy costs sustain uncertainty (ABC News/AP). INFERENCE FLAG: everything beyond that is my read of his record. Warsh's long-standing public position — as a dissenting FOMC member during the crisis era and as a persistent critic of quantitative easing since — has been that the Fed's balance sheet is too large, that its remit has grown too broad, and that credibility is preserved by acting early on inflation rather than explaining it away. His nomination was read at the time as signalling an aggressive balance-sheet pivot alongside rate normalisation (MarketMinute/FinancialContent), and his public statements since point to tighter inflation discipline, streamlined communication and a more narrowly focused central bank (Chase, ABC News/AP). Apply that framework to the current data and the conclusion is uncomfortable for bulls: Core PCE at 3.41% is 141bp above target, 58% of the PCE basket is rising faster than 3%, and financial conditions per the NFCI are LOOSE at -0.55. A chair who believes credibility is the scarce asset does not cut into that. My base case for the next twelve months is the funds rate unchanged or 25–50bp higher, with the more consequential action on the balance sheet, and no cut unless unemployment breaks meaningfully above 4.2% or payrolls turn negative from the current 57.0k.

The thing to understand about this Fed is that the reaction function has changed, and the market has not fully repriced for it. Warsh was confirmed on 13 May 2026 by 54-45, the most divisive vote in Fed history (Chase), which matters because a chair confirmed on a near-party-line vote has every incentive to prove independence rather than accommodation. He has already done the two things that most distinguish him from his predecessor: held rates at his first meeting despite considerable political expectation of cuts, and abolished forward guidance (PBS, CNN). On the political question he has been explicit — asked whether President Trump had pressed him to cut, he said 'The president never asked me to predetermine, commit, fix, decide on any interest rate decision in any of our discussions, nor would I ever agree to do so' (ABC News/AP). Take that at face value and the 'Fed captured by the White House' trade is simply wrong. The removal of forward guidance is the underpriced structural change, and it is worth spelling out what it does. Under Powell, the Fed pre-committed, and markets front-ran the pre-commitment; volatility was suppressed by construction. Without guidance, every meeting becomes a live event and every data release carries more weight, because the market has to solve for the reaction function in real time instead of reading it off a statement. That should raise the fair value of rate volatility permanently. It has not: VIX sits at 18.58 and credit spreads are at the 4th-to-8th percentile of their history. Meanwhile CNN's framing is that Warsh promised a new vision while his colleagues eyed a hike instead of a cut — a chair who may end up more dovish than his own committee, which is close to the opposite of the caricature. For this week specifically, the setup is a hold that nobody disputes, with all the risk in the language. The 2Y at 4.2966 versus a 3.50–3.75% target range says the front end already leans hawkish; the equity market, with futures closed up 0.65% and a VIX at 18.58, does not. One of those two is going to be repriced by Wednesday afternoon, and given that four mega-caps report in the 48 hours either side of it, the sequencing risk is unusually concentrated.
