Persistence score: 58/100 — The disinflation is real but it is narrow, and the headline core number is the problem. Core PCE at 3.29% year-over-year is running more than a full point above the target and well above Core CPI at 2.50% — that gap is unusual and it is the number the Fed actually targets. That is a genuinely encouraging breadth signal, and it is why the computed sub-score sits closer to the middle of its range than to the top. But two things stop this from being an all-clear. First, flexible CPI at 4.68% is running nearly two points above the sticky series and is the only measure in the panel with a meaningfully positive z-score — the volatile, energy- and goods-sensitive basket is re-accelerating even as the persistent one cools, and with Brent at 91.57 and the Hormuz closure unresolved, that is the wrong component to have hot.
