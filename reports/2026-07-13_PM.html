The post-close print was dominated by an energy shock, not an earnings story. US Central Command launched strikes on Iran for a second consecutive night after the IRGC declared the Strait of Hormuz closed, and crude gapped higher — WTI +9.4% to 78.14 and Brent +9.5% to 83.26. That flowed straight into the tape: the S&P 500 fell 0.79% to 7,515.34, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.55% (its expensive growth names most exposed), and Japan's Nikkei sank 1.92%. Europe was an oasis of calm, closing essentially flat. Under the hood it was a textbook oil-shock rotation — Energy (+3.16%) and defensives (Utilities, Staples, Health Care, Real Estate) rose while Info Tech (-2.07%) led the decliners. The fear gauge (VIX) jumped 14% to 17.2, off a low base. Yields backed up across the curve (10Y +6bp to 4.62%) as the oil spike collided with a Fed under new chair Kevin Warsh that has stopped signaling cuts. Notably, credit refused to flinch: IG and HY spreads sit near historic tights, telling you markets are pricing an inflation/geopolitical event, not a solvency one. Versus the prior session this was a clear de-risking, but an orderly, sector-specific one rather than a broad panic.

🔄Sector Analysis

The message: capital rotated from long-duration growth into value, real assets and defensives — a late-cycle, risk-off posture driven by the energy and rate shock rather than a growth-led cyclical upturn.

The signpost mix — loose conditions, tight spreads, low VIX, still-elevated margin-debt growth — is classic late-cycle: the ingredients for a top are accumulating even if the final speculative spike hasn't arrived.

⚔️Iran War

The US–Iran ceasefire has fully collapsed. US Central Command launched strikes against Iran for a second consecutive night (July 12), targeting military assets after Iran's IRGC declared the Strait of Hormuz closed and following attacks on commercial shipping around July 6–7 that prompted Washington to declare the truce over on July 7. Iran retaliated by striking US military facilities in the Gulf — the IRGC reported hitting helicopter maintenance, a hangar housing a P-8 Poseidon and a drone command center at a US air base in Bahrain (Sheikh Isa); sirens sounded in Bahrain, Kuwait reported 'hostile aerial targets,' and Jordan intercepted missiles in its airspace. Shipping through Hormuz has thinned dramatically (roughly a dozen vessels transiting per 24 hours versus ~110/day pre-war). The market impact is concentrated in energy: WTI +9.4% to 78.14 and Brent +9.5% to 83.26, with a Hormuz-closure tail risk keeping a war premium in crude and a bid under defensives.
