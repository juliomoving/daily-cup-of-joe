Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The dot-com analog anchored at the 1990 and 1994 bottoms shows the current cycle (indexed from Oct-2022) at ~238 — extended but far below the dot-com terminal blow-off near 1,400/665 on those same scales. The dotcom drawdown that followed the March-2000 peak was -78% over 31 months; nothing in today's computed signposts argues that magnitude is imminent. The real tell is dispersion: the broad valuation gauge sits at the 23rd percentile of its march-to-peak while semis are already past their old peak. Concentration (top-10 ~39% of the S&P, analyst estimate), CAPE ~39x, market-cap/GDP ~205% and elevated long-term EPS-growth expectations (~70th pct, a BofA bear signpost) all say 'rich,' but the catalyst cluster — compressed credit, easy financial conditions, an un-inverted curve — is what would convert rich into a top. Watch the AI/semis leadership and credit spreads; that is where 2000 rhymes.

⚔️Iran War

The 2026 Iran war (Feb 28–Jun 17, 2026) has formally ended. President Trump and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian signed a memorandum on June 17 setting a 60-day window for a nuclear agreement, with Iran reaffirming it will not pursue a nuclear weapon. The deal provides for a cessation of hostilities in Lebanon, an end to Iranian restrictions on the Strait of Hormuz, a drawdown of US military assets from the region, sanctions relief and a reconstruction/development commitment. As of June 21–22, technical talks continue and friction remains — chiefly over Israeli operations in Lebanon and whether the agreement obliges an Israeli withdrawal — so implementation risk persists even as the shooting war is over. This is the proximate driver of today's oil collapse (WTI -2.62%, Brent -3.25%) as the Hormuz war-premium unwinds.
