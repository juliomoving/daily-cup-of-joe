Friday's close belonged to one man. Kevin Warsh used his 100th-day Jackson Hole keynote to say the US economy is at full employment while inflation readings are 'more concerning,' and pointedly refused to offer forward guidance or a reaction function — 'we should not indulge a regime in which market participants are looking primarily to the Fed for their next trade' (federalreserve.gov, CNBC, 28-Aug-2026). The market did the repricing for him. The US 2-year yield jumped 11.1bp to 4.3434% — by far the largest move on the board — against +4.2bp on the 10-year to 4.718% and only +1.3bp on the 30-year to 5.2056%, a classic hawkish bear-flattener that squeezed 2s10s to a computed 37.46bp. Everything that lives on cheap front-end money got hit: Russell 2000 -1.39% to 2,972.37, Nasdaq Composite -0.52% to 26,402.42, Info Tech -1.29% and Utilities -1.14% on the day. The S&P 500 got off lightly at -0.25% (7,711.76) and the Dow was essentially flat at -0.02%, because the damage was concentrated, not broad. Real-asset hedges took the brunt: gold -3.13% to 4,455.11 and silver -4.14% to 66.3815, with the dollar index up 0.52% to 99.677. Versus the prior session this is a sharp mood shift — Thursday's tape was still leaning into rate relief; Friday's took it away. Yet two things refused to cooperate with the risk-off story. Europe and Japan closed higher (Euro Stoxx 50 +0.95%, STOXX 600 +0.51%, FTSE 100 +0.29%, Nikkei +0.41%) on data that pre-dated the speech, and credit simply did not care: US IG OAS 0.78% (-1bp), US HY 2.63% (-2bp), EUR HY 2.78% (-3bp), EM USD 1.55% (-4bp) — all tighter, all sitting in the bottom few percentiles of their own history. VIX fell to 14.43. Sentiment, then, is not fear; it is a narrow, mechanical de-rating of duration and gold with the risk-appetite machinery still running at full tilt. That gap — a Fed chair telling you he will not rescue you, and a credit market priced as though he will — is the single most important thing on this page.

The key levels are close and worth writing down: US IG record tight is 0.71%, just 7bp away; US HY record tight is 2.33%, just 30bp away; EM record tight is 1.39%, 16bp away. What would it take to get there? A soft-landing confirmation — a benign 4-Sep payroll print with unemployment holding near 4.1% and no wage acceleration — plus continued heavy demand for yield would grind IG through 0.75% and HY into the low 2.40s within weeks; there is nothing structural stopping it. What would it take to go the other way? The honest answer is that at these levels spreads do not need a recession to widen 100bp; they need only a repricing of the front end that persists. Today's 11.1bp move in the 2-year is exactly that seed. My working levels for concern: US HY above 3.00% signals the complacency is cracking, above 3.50% signals a genuine credit-cycle turn, and IG above 1.00% would mean the investment-grade bid — the load-bearing wall of this market — has stepped back. Until one of those prints, credit is telling you there is no problem, and credit at the 3rd percentile has historically been a poor forecaster of its own future.
