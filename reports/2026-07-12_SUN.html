“Hormuz burns, futures bleed: crude +4% and US index futures lower as the Iran ceasefire collapses into a third round of strikes”

Sunday night's tape is being written in the Strait of Hormuz, not on Wall Street. Friday's cash close was constructive — the S&P 500 finished at 7,575.39 (+0.42%, +31.75), the Nasdaq Composite at 26,281.61 (+0.29%) and the Dow at 52,637.01 (+0.29%), with the VIX crushed 5.1% to 15.03 — but the Russell 2000 already dissented (-0.49%), a small hint that the rally's internals were narrowing before the weekend. Since then, the Trump administration's ceasefire with Iran has broken down and US forces have carried out repeated strike rounds after vessel attacks in the Strait (CNN, Jul 10-11). Futures are pricing that risk in real time: ES -0.32% to 7,595.75, NQ -0.67% to 29,831.50, DM -0.26%, and the 10Y note future -4.5/32nds — bonds are NOT rallying, which is the single most important tell of the session. Energy is doing the flight-to-safety instead: WTI +4.03% to $74.29 and Brent +3.85% to $78.94. The dollar is bid (DXY +0.19% to 101.148, EUR/USD -0.20%, USD/JPY 162.14), while gold is DOWN 1.21% to $4,070.27 and silver -2.17%. A war-risk shock that lifts oil and the dollar but sells gold and Treasuries is not a classic geopolitical risk-off — it is an inflation shock, and the market is treating it as one. That matters enormously into Tuesday's June CPI print, which lands the same morning as JPMorgan, Goldman, BofA, Citi and Wells Fargo report, with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh testifying to Congress in the same week. Credit is asleep at the wheel: US IG OAS 76bp, HY 263bp, both in the bottom 3% of their historical range. Positioning is long, complacent, and levered into a supply shock.
