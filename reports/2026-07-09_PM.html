Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

Valuation marker: today's Nasdaq P/E (40x) ≈ 23% of the ~175x 2000-peak P/E.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The dot-com overlay is instructive: today's cycle (anchored Oct-2022, +138% and running) sits below the vertical 1999-2000 ascent in slope, and today's valuation marker is only ~23% of the way up that historical froth path — the index is expensive but not parabolic. The danger isn't the P/E multiple; it's the combination of maxed-out complacency (VIX 15.84, credit at 1st-percentile tights, loose NFCI) with narrow semis leadership. That is precisely the configuration in which a death-cross in the Nasdaq — historically a ~60% trend-change signal — would do real damage. Concentration (~39% top-10, analyst estimate), a ~40x CAPE (analyst estimate) and ~214% market-cap/GDP (analyst estimate) are all near-record, but elevated long-term EPS-growth expectations (~68th percentile, analyst estimate) and only moderate IPO froth (~58, analyst estimate) say the mania is institutional and AI-narrative-driven, not the retail free-for-all of 2000. Verdict: late-cycle froth to be respected and hedged, not a confirmed top.
