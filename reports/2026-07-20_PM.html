NEAR TERM

Most likely a hold at the next meeting, with a hawkish, hike-leaning bias. ~1 YEAR

Directionally lower rates are Warsh's stated ambition, but the ~1-year path is constrained by elevated inflation. Expect structural reform (five task forces on communications, data and the inflation framework, plus a smaller balance sheet) to advance faster than actual easing; meaningful cuts likely require a clear inflation cooldown that the current data (Core PCE 3.41%) do not yet show. (Inference — flag as uncertain.)

Warsh took the chair promising 'a new vision' for the Fed and was appointed with a mandate to lower rates, but he inherits a sticky-inflation problem worsened by the Iran war. His committee has held four straight meetings and even hinted at a possible hike. The tension between his easing preference and the inflation reality is the defining Fed story of 2026; near-term he is effectively boxed in.
