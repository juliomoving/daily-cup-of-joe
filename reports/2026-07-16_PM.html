Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

Valuation marker: today's Nasdaq P/E (40x) ≈ 23% of the ~175x 2000-peak P/E.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The dot-com drawdown reference (peak 5,048.62 in Mar-2000 to trough 1,114.11 in Oct-2002, -78% over 31 months) is a reminder of the downside if froth unwinds — but the shape of today's risk is different. In 2000 the mania was in price and breadth; today the extremes are in how little investors are being paid to take risk (spreads at 2nd–4th percentile, VIX 16.7) and in one narrow group (semis at 85% of peak valuation). The valuation marker sits at just the 23rd percentile of the froth path with the current index P/E at ~39.7x versus a ~175x peak-era analog. The bearish signposts to watch — margin-debt growth (56% of peak), AAII net-bulls (79%), SLOOS tightening (89%) — are elevated but not yet triggering. Translation: fragile-but-not-bursting. The catalyst most likely to matter is a repricing of AI-semis earnings (hence Intel and Alphabet next week) or a credit/vol shock that ends the complacency.
