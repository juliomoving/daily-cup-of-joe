That directional mix — mostly falling, none rising — is the encouraging part. The discouraging part is Flexible CPI at 5.06%, the 77th percentile with a z-score of 0.56 and the only measure rising: the energy- and commodity-sensitive components of the basket are the ones running hot, which is exactly what a Middle East war premium produces and exactly what today's 6.6% crude decline attacks. Breadth is the third leg. On the Dallas Fed distribution, 28.1% of the PCE basket is outright falling and another 24.3% is rising between 0% and 2% — so 52.4% of the basket is at or below target. But 23.5% of the basket is rising faster than 5% a year (15.4% in the 5–10% bucket plus 8.1% above 10%), and that fat right tail is why I will not call this beaten. Market expectations are notably split: the 5-year breakeven at 2.25% is close to target-consistent, while the 1-year breakeven at 1.62% is materially below it — investors are betting the near-term energy shock reverses hard, which is precisely the trade today's headline validates. On the wage side, Average Hourly Earnings at 3.5% is consistent with roughly 2% inflation given trend productivity, and ECI at 0.9% is not a source of pressure. Net: the persistent, wage-driven, broad-based inflation problem is not the risk here. The risk is that the fat tail of fast-rising categories re-ignites if the Hormuz negotiation fails. Verdict — disinflationary drift with a live energy tail, and a Fed that has no reason to cut and every reason to wait.
