European IG/HY and EM USD spreads are all in the same 4th-7th percentile, i.e. historically very tight and miles from any crisis marker (HY's historical max is ~1,970bps versus 276 today). Practically, that means investors are being paid almost nothing extra to hold corporate risk over Treasuries. Key levels to watch: HY OAS drifting through ~325-350bps would be the first sign the equity stress is bleeding into credit; a move toward 450-500bps would signal genuine risk repricing. The conditions to get there would be a Hormuz oil shock, a disorderly tech de-rating that hits leveraged issuers, or a hawkish Warsh surprise tightening financial conditions. None of that is in the price yet — credit complacency is the risk, not credit distress.

Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

Valuation marker: today's Nasdaq P/E (124x) ≈ 71% of the ~175x 2000-peak P/E.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The bubble math is a tale of two markets. On the 'this-is-2000-again' side: a Shiller CAPE around 41x (near its all-time high of ~44x), a Buffett market-cap-to-GDP ratio near 237% (strongly overvalued, ~2 standard deviations rich), S&P top-10 concentration near record levels (~38% analyst estimate, with the seven largest names alone ~a third of the index), long-term EPS-growth expectations elevated by the AI capex boom (analyst estimate ~70/100), and credit/vol complacency that actually surpasses the dot-com peak. On the 'not-yet' side: price stretch above trend is modest, IPO froth is warm but not manic (analyst estimate ~55/100 versus the 2000 frenzy), and sentiment/breadth are cautious rather than euphoric. The tell this week is behavioral: Comm Services and Discretionary fell ~5% in a single session, which is exactly how an over-owned, over-valued leadership group starts to crack. The set-up is fragile-and-expensive rather than blow-off-and-euphoric — the danger is a valuation air-pocket into an unforgiving earnings week, not a 2000-style mania still building.
