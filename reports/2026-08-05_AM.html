Computed readings: US IG OAS 76bps, z-score -0.85, 2nd percentile of its own history (range 71 to 618bps) — meaning investment-grade lenders have demanded less compensation for corporate risk than today only about 2% of the time on record, and the all-time tight is a mere 5bps away. US HY OAS 266bps, z-score -0.91, 4th percentile (range 233 to 1,971bps) — 33bps from the record tight, 1,705bps from the wide. EUR IG 78bps (7th percentile), EUR HY 290bps (11th percentile), and EM USD 166bps at the 4th percentile with the most extreme z-score on the board at -1.33 (range 139 to 1,087bps) — emerging-market dollar debt is priced for a world with no dollar-funding stress and no Hormuz closure. And it is still tightening: IG -1bp, HY -5bps overnight, with EUR HY +1bp and EM +2bps the only nominal widening. The crisis-percentile columns read zero across GFC 2008, Euro Debt 2011, COVID 2020 and Hike 2022 for every index — today's spreads are nowhere near any of those episodes, which is the point: there is no cushion. Key levels and what it takes to reach them. IG 71bps is the record and the realistic floor — reaching it needs nothing more than continued index-fund inflows and light supply, a matter of weeks. IG 100bps is the first real repricing and requires either a genuine growth scare (ISM Manufacturing, computed at 55.6, breaking below 50) or a funding accident. HY 233bps is the record tight and is achievable on a Hormuz de-escalation headline alone. The asymmetry lives on the other side: HY 320-350bps is roughly one bad payroll print plus an oil spike away — recall payrolls at 57k are already soft and initial claims at 197k are not; HY 450bps requires an actual default-cycle repricing, which needs the SLOOS bank-tightening measure (computed at 0.0% net tightening today) to turn materially positive. From 266bps, you are risking 33bps of carry-adjusted upside against 60-90bps of realistic downside, and multiples of that in a tail. The trade is not to be short credit for a living; it is to stop being paid to be long it and to buy the convexity while it is this cheap.

🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 60/100 — Sticky, not accelerating — and stickier than the headline suggests. Core PCE at 3.29% year-over-year is the number that matters for the Fed and it is well above the 2% target; Core CPI at 2.60% flatters the picture. The computed core-trend measures cluster tightly in the 2.2-2.8% range (Median CPI 2.71%, 16% Trimmed-Mean CPI 2.63%, Median PCE 2.66%, Trimmed-Mean PCE 2.23%, Core Sticky CPI 2.81%) and their computed direction tags are mostly 'falling' or 'flat' — the underlying trend is drifting the right way, slowly. The complication is breadth and tails. The computed Flexible CPI measure runs at 5.06%, is tagged 'rising', and sits at the 77th percentile with the only meaningfully positive z-score in the panel at 0.56 — the volatile, energy- and goods-sensitive part of the basket is reheating, and with Brent at 80.37 and rising, that is unlikely to be transitory this quarter. The Dallas Fed distribution corroborates: 8.1% of the PCE basket is rising faster than 10% and 15.4% between 5% and 10% — so 23.5% of consumption is inflating above 5%, and 29.3% above 3%, against 24.3% inflating between 0-2% and 28.1% outright falling. That is a bimodal basket, not a converged one. The market disagrees with the data and sides with disinflation: the 5-year breakeven at 2.21% and the 1-year at 1.60% say investors expect target-consistent inflation within a year — a bet that the flexible component rolls over. Wages are the quiet good news: average hourly earnings at 3.50% are consistent with 2% inflation given trend productivity, and the ECI reading of 0.90% shows no wage-price spiral. Labour is loosening without breaking (unemployment 4.2%, JOLTS openings 7,359k, quits at 2.2%, payrolls at just 57k, but initial claims at only 197k — firms have stopped hiring, not started firing). Bottom line: this is a 2.5-3% inflation economy pretending to be a 2% one, with an energy-shaped upside risk sitting in the Gulf. That is enough to keep Warsh patient and enough to keep the 10-year at 4.62%.
