IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The uncomfortable feature of this market is not that it looks like March 2000 — it is that it looks like March 2000 in exactly the places that matter for the downside and nothing like it in the places that would normally warn you first. In 2000 the sequence was: price detaches from trend, retail piles in, the curve inverts, credit cracks, then equities fall 78% over 31 months (Nasdaq peak 5048.62 on 10-Mar-2000 to trough 1114.11 on 09-Oct-2002). Today the credit and complacency legs are already more extreme than the 2000 condition — IG at 134%, Baa-10Y at 149%, VIX at 200%, financial conditions at 165% — while the two legs that historically fire early are absent: price stretch at 15% and the ISM rollover at 0-17%. Sentiment is the strongest genuine argument against the bubble call. The AAII net-bullish spread is negative at -11.5pp and consumer confidence is 90.2. Manias do not run with a bearish retail base. What that means practically is that the top-down bubble frame is the wrong tool for timing and the right tool for sizing. The path from here does not need euphoria; it needs a catalyst that repricings the credit complacency, and there is a live candidate on the screen every morning — Brent at 88.75 with Hormuz closed. The current cycle, anchored at the October-2022 low, is 46 months in and sits at 237.8 on a rebased index; the 1990-anchored dot-com analogue was at roughly 1424 at its month-113 peak. Different scale, different slope, similar dependency on a single leadership group. Own the trend, but do not pretend the credit market is giving you a discount for the risk — it is charging you a premium to take it.
