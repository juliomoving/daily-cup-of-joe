Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

Valuation marker: today's Nasdaq P/E (84x) ≈ 48% of the ~175x 2000-peak P/E.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The bubble debate is really two debates. Valuation hawks point to CAPE ~41.4, a Buffett ratio ~230%, and the most concentrated index on record (~37% top-10) — all unambiguously expensive. But the classic 2000 tells are uneven: retail euphoria is absent (AAII deeply net-bearish, subdued consumer confidence), and the Nasdaq's price stretch and headline P/E are nowhere near 2000. What rhymes most with a late cycle is the complacency stack — record-tight credit, the loosest financial conditions in the sample, and a VIX at 16 — which is exactly what tends to precede a froth-unwind rather than coincide with the top. Wednesday's -2.32% Info Tech drop and the 100th-percentile stretch in the indices are the proximate warning; the absence of broad retail mania is the reason this looks more like a concentrated-leadership wobble than an imminent 2000-style collapse.
