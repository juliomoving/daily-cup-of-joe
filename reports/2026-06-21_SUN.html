Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

Valuation marker: today's Nasdaq P/E (41x) ≈ 23% of the ~175x 2000-peak P/E.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The 2000 analog matters because of the drawdown it produced — the Nasdaq fell 78% over 31 months from the Mar-2000 peak. Today's risk is not that headline P/Es match 2000 (they don't), but that the same late-cycle catalysts are flashing — a re-steepening 2s10s (now +27.7bp from inverted), rolled-over ISMs, record-tight credit and rock-bottom VIX — while leadership narrows into semis on an AI capex story that earnings (Micron, Wednesday) will now have to validate. My analyst-estimate inputs: S&P top-10 weight ~39%, Shiller CAPE ~39x, market-cap/GDP (Buffett) ~205%, long-term EPS-growth expectations elevated (~70/100), and IPO froth still moderate (~35/100) — a quiet new-issue market is the single clearest piece of evidence this is not 2000.

🏦Fed Chair Warsh
NEAR TERM

Hold, with a hawkish bias (inferred from this month's decision). At his first meeting as chair the FOMC held the benchmark at 3.50–3.75% for a fourth consecutive meeting, and the June dot-plot's median moved higher for 2026 — implying the balance of risks tilts toward a hike rather than a cut, especially with energy prices adding upward inflation pressure. Expect the next meeting to hold again unless inflation data force the hike the dots hint at; near-term cuts look off the table.

~1 YEAR

A higher-for-longer regime with a restructured Fed (inferred from his on-record actions and long-standing views). Warsh has dropped formal forward guidance ('not well-suited to the current policy conjuncture'), wants fewer public speeches and more closed-door debate, and launched five task forces to review communications, the balance sheet, data sources, productivity/jobs and the inflation framework. Consistent with his long-held hawkish, rules-and-credibility-oriented stance, the most likely 1-year path is rates held restrictive (cuts only on clear disinflation or a labor break), continued balance-sheet runoff, and a leaner, less-telegraphed Fed. Flagged as inference from his statements and writings, not a committed path.

Warsh is reshaping both the message and the machinery of the Fed. The substantive signal — a hawkish hold with a hike-leaning dot-plot despite political pressure for cuts — pairs with a structural overhaul (no forward guidance, fewer speeches, five review task forces). For markets that means less hand-holding, more data-dependence, and a higher bar for easing; the back-up in the 2Y to 4.21% and tight financial conditions are the tape pricing exactly that.
