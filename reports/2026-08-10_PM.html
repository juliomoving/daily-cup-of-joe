The tape looked quiet and wasn't. The S&P 500 closed at 7,753.11, down just 4.53 points (-0.06%), but underneath that flat print was a full energy shock: WTI jumped 5.05% to $82.13 and Brent 5.13% to $87.84 as the Strait of Hormuz stayed shut and Iran-backed Houthi forces struck Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery over the weekend. Nat gas rose 4.96% to $2.794. The S&P energy sector gained 4.63% on the day and is now +31.59% year-to-date, the best group in the market. That crude move fed straight into the rates complex: the US 10-year rose 6.13bp to 4.7067%, the 2-year 4.58bp to 4.241%, the 30-year 5.02bp to 5.251%, with Bunds +4.7bp, Gilts +6.8bp and JGBs +2.3bp all following. Higher discount rates hit the long-duration parts of the equity market — Nasdaq -0.32%, Info Tech -1.10%, Real Estate -1.23%, Utilities -1.11% — while Russell 2000 fell 0.56% and VIX rose 3.76% to 15.46. Health Care (+1.68%) was the second-best group. Versus the prior session the character of the market changed more than the index level: this was a rotation out of rate-sensitive growth and into the inflation trade, not a broad de-risking. Japan was the outlier, with the Nikkei up 2.08% (+1,363.51) to 66,970.22; Europe was flat (SX5E +0.18%, SXXP +0.03%) and the FTSE 100 fell 0.35%. Credit did not blink — US IG OAS unchanged at 0.77%, HY 2.64% (+0.01), EM 1.62% unchanged, and European HY actually tightened 5bp to 2.81%. FX was near-comatose apart from a 0.28% DXY gain to 99.814. Sentiment reads as complacent-but-twitchy: nobody wants to sell equities into a war-driven commodity rally, and nobody is being paid to hedge it either.

🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 64/100 — Sticky, not spiralling — and today's oil move just removed the disinflation escape route. The core-trend measures are clustered tightly in the mid-2s and going nowhere: Median CPI 2.71% YoY, 16% trimmed-mean CPI 2.63%, Median PCE 2.66%, trimmed-mean PCE 2.23%, sticky CPI 2.84% and core sticky CPI 2.81%. Every one of those sits in the 56th-70th percentile of its own history with z-scores between -0.12 and +0.09 — statistically ordinary readings that happen to be a half-point too high, with directions split between 'flat' and 'falling'. That is a plateau, not a descent. The headline problem is Flexible CPI at 5.06%, a 77th-percentile reading with a +0.56 z-score and the only measure tagged 'rising' — flexible prices are the energy- and commodity-sensitive component, and it was already accelerating before Brent added 5.13% today. Breadth from the Dallas Fed distribution is genuinely two-sided: 28.1% of the PCE basket is outright falling and another 24.3% is rising between zero and 2%, so 52.4% of the basket is behaving consistently with target — but 23.5% is still running above 5%, including 8.1% above 10%. That fat right tail is what keeps Core PCE at 3.29% YoY even while Core CPI has come down to 2.60%. Wages are no longer the driver: average hourly earnings at 3.2% and ECI at 0.9% are consistent with 2% inflation, and with nonfarm payrolls at -23k and unemployment at 4.1%, labour is disinflationary from here. Markets are relaxed — the 5-year breakeven is 2.2661% and the 1-year only 1.7781%, so the bond market is pricing this energy shock as transitory. My read: they are roughly right on the medium term and complacent on the next two prints. The verdict is persistence without acceleration — inflation settles in a 2.5-3% band rather than returning to 2%, and the Fed's problem is that this is exactly the level at which you can neither cut nor hike comfortably.
