🏦Fed Chair Warsh
NEAR TERM

Hawkish hold. At his first meeting as chair (Jun 16–17), Warsh's FOMC unanimously held the target range at 3.50–3.75%, but the dots were hawkish: nine members signaled support for higher rates this year and six of those backed two quarter-point hikes. Base case for the next meeting is another hold with a hawkish bias; a hike is a live risk if the sticky core inflation prints above do not cool. (Inference from the June statement/dots.)

~1 YEAR

Bias to the upside on rates relative to the prior regime — Warsh has emphasized re-establishing the Fed's credibility to 'deliver price stability' and explicitly sees no case to revisit the 2% target until that is achieved. Over ~12 months expect either an extended hold or one-to-two hikes if inflation persistence continues, with cuts unlikely absent a clear growth/labor deterioration. (Inference; the gold/silver bid and bear-steepening front end are consistent with the market taking the hawkish signal seriously.)

Warsh is reshaping the institution as much as the rate path: he is standing up five task forces (communications, data sources, inflation frameworks), wants fewer policymaker speeches and more closed-door debate, and has shortened the policy statement to stress price stability. The communications shift means less forward guidance and more emphasis on the inflation mandate — hawkish in tone even when the rate decision is a hold.
