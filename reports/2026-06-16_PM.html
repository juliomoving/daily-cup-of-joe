Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

Valuation marker: today's Nasdaq P/E (41x) ≈ 24% of the ~175x 2000-peak P/E.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

This is a valuation-and-concentration problem more than a sentiment bubble. On the froth axis, only semiconductors approach dot-com extremes; the broad index multiple is rich (CAPE near record) but the runaway-IPO, retail-frenzy, blow-off-top texture of early 2000 is largely absent — IPO froth is elevated (~66/100, with SpaceX's record $75bn deal and AI mega-listings queuing) but breadth-of-mania is narrower. On the catalyst axis, the danger is complacency: spreads at all-time tights and an ultra-loose NFCI mean the system is primed to amplify any shock, not that a shock is imminent. The reference drawdown is sobering — the 2000–02 Nasdaq fell -78% over 31 months — but the present setup, with positive sentiment readings and an un-inverted curve, looks more 'expensive late-cycle' than 'peak bubble.' Watch the semis multiple, long-term EPS-growth expectations (elevated, ~70/100, a BofA bear signpost), and the first credit-spread widening as the tells that the regime is turning.
