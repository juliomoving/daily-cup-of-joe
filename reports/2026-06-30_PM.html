📋PM Edition — What You Need to Know

“Quarter Ends With a Melt-Up: Nasdaq Leads, Curve Steepens, Spreads at the Floor — But the Bear-Market Signposts Are Quietly Flashing”

US equities closed the quarter on a risk-on note, with every major index green and tech doing the heavy lifting. The S&P 500 rose +0.79% to a record-stretch 7,499.36 (+58.93) and the Nasdaq Composite jumped +1.52% to 26,213.72 (+393.58) as Info Tech (+2.55% on the day) reasserted leadership; the Dow (+0.26%) and Russell 2000 (+0.46%) lagged, a narrow-breadth tape. The VIX collapsed -6.8% to 16.45, the picture of complacency. The day's bigger tell was rates: the long end sold off hard — 10Y +9.1bp to 4.465%, 30Y +8.8bp to 4.951%, 2Y +6.8bp to 4.172% — re-steepening 2s10s to a positive +29bp even as stocks rallied, a 'good-news/term-premium' move rather than a flight to safety. Credit refuses to blink: US IG OAS sits at 74bp (1st percentile vs history) and HY at 277bp (7th percentile), about as tight as they have ever traded. Crude slipped (WTI 69.5) despite a still-live, fragile US-Iran ceasefire, gold idled at 4,008 below a death-crossed 200DMA, and the dollar firmed marginally (DXY 101.17). Versus the prior session this is a continuation of the melt-up, with sentiment euphoric on the surface and the late-cycle/signpost gauges — low vol, tight credit, loose financial conditions — increasingly extended underneath.

Until one of those lands, carry stays the path of least resistance.

Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

Valuation marker: today's Nasdaq P/E (41x) ≈ 23% of the ~175x 2000-peak P/E.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The dangerous configuration isn't that any single froth gauge is screaming — it's the combination: a 16-handle VIX, 1st-percentile credit spreads, the loosest financial conditions of the cycle, and a 100th-percentile, +3 z-score Nasdaq, all while semis trade at a richer multiple than the dot-com peak. Valuation (CAPE ~40, Buffett ~230%) gives the bears their long-run case; the signpost cluster (low vol, tight credit, loose NFCI, elevated margin debt growth at +40.7% YoY) gives them the short-run trigger list. What's notably NOT 2000 is the IPO market (orderly, not a mania) and the still-positive yield curve (+29bp 2s10s, ~70% of the way to the inverted 2000 setup). The honest read: this looks like a late-stage bull that can run further on momentum but is pricing in essentially no risk premium — the asymmetry favors trimming beta and buying cheap volatility, not calling the top.

⚔️Iran War

The June US-Iran ceasefire/memorandum of understanding (announced mid-June, with Trump lifting the Strait of Hormuz naval blockade) remains in place but visibly fragile. In the latest days, Trump threatened renewed military action accusing Tehran of ceasefire violations; the US has struck Iran in tit-for-tat exchanges after fire on a vessel in the Strait of Hormuz, and Iran has called those US strikes a 'clear violation' of the June 18 MoU.
