Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

Valuation marker: today's Nasdaq P/E (40x) ≈ 23% of the ~175x 2000-peak P/E.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The froth is concentrated, not generalized. Analyst-estimated S&P top-10 weight near 38% and a Shiller CAPE around 39x and market-cap/GDP (Buffett) near 205% are all rich versus history but below the 2000 valuation extreme on the broad index, even as the semis sit in their own mini-bubble. Long-term EPS-growth expectations (analyst estimate ~70/100) are elevated — a classic BofA bear-market signpost — and IPO froth (~55/100) is warming but not manic. The market's vulnerability is therefore not that everything is priced like 2000; it is that complacency indicators (VIX, put/call, credit, financial conditions) have left no room for error while leadership has narrowed into semis. A shock doesn't need a valuation reason to bite — the signposts are the bite radius.
