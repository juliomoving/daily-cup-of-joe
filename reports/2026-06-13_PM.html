“Risk Rips Into the Weekend as War Premium Drains From Oil — Cyclicals Lead, VIX Crushed, Credit at Historic Tights”

The Friday Jun 12 close was a broad, almost defiant risk-on session: the S&P 500 finished +0.50% at 7,431.46, the Dow +0.70%, the Russell 2000 +0.79%, and VIX collapsed 9.1% to 17.68 — all while a hot US/Israel–Iran war ground on. The tell was crude: WTI fell 3.23% to 84.88 and Brent 3.37% to 87.33, the market draining geopolitical risk premium rather than pricing escalation. Leadership rotated hard into cyclicals and value — Materials +1.83%, Financials +1.35%, Utilities +1.12%, Real Estate +0.95% — with mega-cap Tech (+0.37%) a laggard, a genuine broadening. Europe and Japan outran the US outright (Euro Stoxx +2.16%, Nikkei +2.81%, FTSE +1.63%). Rates sold off modestly (10Y +1.8bp to 4.479%, 2Y to 4.081%) as global core yields eased. Credit is the loudest complacency signal: US IG OAS 0.72% (z −0.90, 0th percentile) and HY 2.66% (z −0.91, 3rd) sit at or near the tightest in their histories. Net: euphoric, late-cycle price action layered over sticky inflation and an active war — a market priced for everything to go right.

🏦Fed Chair Warsh
NEAR TERM

No move at his first meeting as chair. Consensus is the Fed holds the funds rate (reported ~3.50–3.75%) and shifts the guidance from an easing bias toward an explicit neutral stance — with sticky inflation (Core PCE 3.29%), the live debate is even tilting toward whether the next move could be a hike rather than a cut.

~1 YEAR

On hold-to-restrictive through 2026 on the policy rate, paired with the structural agenda he has championed for years: a concrete target and timeline to shrink the ~$6.7tn balance sheet (his stated 'regime change'), tighter restrictions on the composition of Fed holdings, reforms to inflation measurement, and changes to FOMC communication. He is regarded as inflation-hawkish but has signaled near-term continuity; cuts would require clearer disinflation than the current sticky trend provides. (Inference from his on-record positions, flagged as such.)

Warsh was confirmed 54–45 on May 13, 2026 (the most divisive Fed vote on record) and sworn in May 22. His long-standing writings emphasize a smaller balance sheet, skepticism of QE, and concern about Fed independence/credibility — pointing to a chair more inclined to lean against premature easing than to deliver the cuts some associate with his name.
