🏦Fed Chair Warsh
NEAR TERM

Hold / hawkish bias. Warsh chaired his first FOMC this week and left rates unchanged, with the statement and dot path signaling no cuts in 2026 and nearly half the committee open to a hike later this year — consistent with his long-standing inflation-credibility focus. Expect the same posture at the next meeting absent a clear growth or labor downturn.

~1 YEAR

Gradual easing, data-dependent — markets are positioned for maybe one to two cuts over the next year rather than an imminent cutting cycle. Warsh's stated agenda is 'lower rates over time' paired with a smaller balance sheet, so the one-year path is a slow normalization contingent on the sticky core measures (Core PCE 3.29%) cooling.

Beyond rates, Warsh announced a 'regime change' for the institution: five task forces covering core areas of monetary policy, fewer official speeches, criticism of over-reliance on forward guidance and the SEP dots, and a rethink of how the Fed measures inflation.
