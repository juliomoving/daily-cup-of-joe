The Thursday close belonged to the bond market, and equities simply took the gift. The US curve bull-steepened across the board — 2-year down 5.9bp to 4.1424%, 10-year down 5.0bp to 4.6427%, 30-year down 4.5bp to 5.2125% — with the front end leading, which is the market quietly pricing a softer labour backdrop (payrolls at −23k, unemployment 4.1%) rather than a dovish Fed pivot, since Warsh has explicitly withdrawn forward guidance. Risk assets read it as fuel: S&P 500 +0.65% to 7798.99, Nasdaq Composite +0.81% to 26803.03, Dow +0.13%, Russell 2000 +0.24%. Japan was the standout, Nikkei +1.16% to 68308.59 even as the 10-year JGB backed up 2.8bp to 2.885% — the one major curve going the other way. Europe was a shrug (Euro Stoxx 50 +0.18%, STOXX 600 −0.04%) and the UK the session's loser, FTSE 100 −0.56% with gilts only 1.7bp richer. Versus the prior session this is continuation, not rotation: the same leadership, the same absence of volatility, VIX up a rounding-error 0.08 to 14.63. Sentiment is best described as unbothered. Credit did not move — US IG OAS pinned at 78bp, HY 1bp tighter at 266bp — and both sit in the 4th percentile of their own history, which is the single most important fact in this report. Commodities were quiet at the margin (WTI $81.11, −0.17%; gold $4,353.41, +0.07%) even with the Strait of Hormuz effectively shut and the IEA warning that global oil stocks are drawing down fast. That disconnect — a live energy chokepoint, a shrinking payroll count, and credit priced for perfection — is the story. The melt-up is real and technically confirmed; the margin for error underneath it is not.

Credit is the weakest link in this market precisely because it looks the strongest. US IG OAS closed unchanged at 78bp, US HY 1bp tighter at 266bp. The computed statistics are the point: IG carries a z-score of −0.82 and sits in the 4th percentile of its own history — tighter than 96% of all observations ever recorded, against a historical range of 71bp to 618bp. HY is identical in character: z-score −0.90, 4th percentile, range 233bp to 1971bp. Europe is barely different (EUR IG 77bp, z −0.89, 6th percentile; EUR HY 283bp, z −0.72, 9th percentile), and EM USD is the most extreme of all at 162bp with a z-score of −1.36 and a 3rd-percentile rank — the tightest emerging-market risk premium in this dataset's memory, priced while a war closes the world's most important oil route. Every crisis-comparison percentile — GFC 2008, EuroDebt 2011, COVID 2020, the 2022 hiking cycle — reads 0, meaning current spreads bear no resemblance to any stress episode on record. Key levels and what it takes to reach them. IG has 7bp of room to its all-time tight of 71bp; that requires nothing more than continuation — another few weeks of this tape gets there. The asymmetry is on the other side: a move to the 90–100bp area, historically unremarkable, would require only a genuine growth scare (a second negative payroll print, or an ISM roll from 55.6) and would cost long-duration IG holders real money against 78bp of carry. For HY, 300bp is the first line that matters — a 34bp widening, roughly one bad week — and 400bp is where the market would be pricing an actual default cycle; that needs an oil shock through Hormuz feeding into consumer weakness, or a credit event in a levered AI-capex borrower. EM at 162bp is the trade I'd want to be short: a 3rd-percentile spread with a live geopolitical catalyst and a dollar sitting on 100 offers perhaps 20bp of upside against 150bp+ of downside. Bottom line: you are being paid the least in a generation to take credit risk, at the moment the macro tail is fattest.

🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 58/100 — Disinflation is real, broad, and slower than the headline suggests — and the composition is the problem. Every one of the seven underlying measures in the computed core-trend panel is falling, which is a genuinely encouraging signal and rules out re-acceleration as the base case. But 'falling' and 'low' are different claims. Median CPI is 2.7%, 16% trimmed-mean CPI 2.6%, median PCE 2.66%, trimmed-mean PCE 2.23%, core sticky CPI 2.72% — a cluster sitting a half-point above target, not at it. The gap between core CPI at 2.5% and core PCE at 3.29% is unusually wide, and where the two disagree the trimmed and median measures side with persistence. The Dallas Fed breadth distribution is where the comfort runs out: 8.1% of the PCE basket is rising faster than 10% and another 15.4% is running 5–10%, so nearly a quarter of consumer spending is experiencing inflation at more than double target, while only 28.1% is outright falling and 24.3% is in the 0–2% zone. That is not a broad-based return to price stability; it is a bimodal economy with a hot tail. Flexible CPI at 4.68% versus core sticky at 2.72% tells you the hot tail is concentrated in volatile, supply-sensitive categories — precisely the ones exposed to a closed Strait of Hormuz. Wages are cooperating (average hourly earnings 3.2%, ECI 0.9%) and markets are relaxed (5-year breakeven 2.22%, 1-year 1.64% — the front end is priced for below-target inflation within a year). My persistence score of 58 says: sticky above target, decisively not spiralling, with the risk asymmetric to the upside because the energy channel is armed and the labour channel is no longer the binding constraint. For a Fed chair who has promised a regime change against inflation and removed forward guidance, this data set justifies doing nothing for a while — which is exactly what I expect.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The useful framing is that 2000 and 2026 are frothy in different places. In 2000 the mania was in price and in the retail investor; today it is in concentration, in credit, and in the complete absence of priced volatility. That matters for how it ends. A price bubble deflates when the marginal buyer runs out — messy, but self-limiting, and the computed drawdown reference shows what that looked like: a 78% fall from 5048.62 on 10 March 2000 to 1114.11 on 9 October 2002, taking 31 months. A concentration-plus-credit setup breaks differently: it needs an external catalyst, and then it transmits through leverage rather than sentiment. Margin debt growing 41.2% year over year, IG spreads more complacent than at the 2000 peak, and a top-10 index weight near 41% mean a shock to two or three names propagates mechanically into index funds, into margin calls, and into credit — without any retail mania needing to break first. The signposts that would tell you the clock has started are specific and currently absent: an ISM rollover from 55.6, a SLOOS reading that turns positive from 0.0%, and IG spreads widening off 78bp. Until those move, the computed evidence says stay invested and stay hedged. The mistake this year is not being long; it is being long without paying for insurance while the VIX sits at 14.63.
