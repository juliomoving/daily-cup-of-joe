🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 58/100 — Inflation is decelerating in its underlying core but is not yet at target, and the composition of what remains is the problem. Core PCE at 3.29% year-over-year is the headline embarrassment — it sits a full 79bp above Core CPI at 2.50%, and it is the measure the Fed's own framework leans on. Underneath it, the trimmed and median family is well-behaved and, critically, every single one of the seven computed core-trend measures is flagged 'falling': Median CPI 2.70%, 16% Trimmed-Mean CPI 2.60%, Median PCE 2.66%, Trimmed-Mean PCE 2.23%, Sticky CPI 2.76%, Core Sticky CPI 2.72%. The computed Core-Trend Sub-Score sits in the middle of its historical range with individual percentile ranks clustered tightly in the 63–65 band and z-scores essentially at zero — a genuinely neutral underlying trend, neither accelerating nor collapsing. The disqualifier is Flexible CPI at 4.68%, with a 75th-percentile rank and a z-score of 0.49 — the volatile, energy- and goods-sensitive component is running nearly double the sticky component. In a normal cycle you would look through that. With Hormuz effectively closed and the IEA warning on stockpiles, you cannot; the flexible component is where an energy shock enters, and it is already the hottest line in the panel. Breadth is the second concern. The Dallas Fed distribution shows 28.1% of the PCE basket outright falling and 24.3% rising at 0–2% — 52.4% of the basket is at or below target, which is genuinely good news. But 23.5% is still rising faster than 5% (8.1% above 10% and 15.4% in the 5–10% band), a fat right tail that a five-quarter-old disinflation should have thinned by now. Wages are cooperating: average hourly earnings at 3.2% and ECI at 0.9% are consistent with 2% inflation given trend productivity, and with payrolls at -23k and unemployment at 4.1% there is no wage-push impulse coming. The market agrees and then some — 5-year breakevens at 2.22% and 1-year breakevens at 1.64% say investors expect the near-term print to undershoot target outright. My read: the disinflation is real but has stalled in the last mile, the risk distribution is asymmetric to the upside because of energy, and anyone extrapolating the trimmed-mean series to 2% by year-end is ignoring where the shock is actually going to arrive. A score of 58 out of 100 reflects moderate persistence — sticky enough to keep the Fed frozen, soft enough that it will not force a hike.
