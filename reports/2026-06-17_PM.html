“Warsh's First Hold Bites Wall Street: Stocks Sink, VIX Pops, Dollar Firms as Fed Signals Higher-for-Longer”

The post-close story is a hawkish handoff: Kevin Warsh chaired his first FOMC and the Fed held but signaled higher rates ahead, with a bloc of members now flagging a possible hike by year-end as Iran-war oil has pushed CPI to a multi-year high. US equities sold the message — S&P 500 -1.21% to 7,420.10, Nasdaq -1.34%, Dow -0.98%, Russell -0.72% — while the VIX jumped +12.4% to 18.44. The front end did the heavy lifting: 2Y +13.3bp to 4.18% and 10Y +4.75bp to 4.49% (a bear-flattener), the dollar firmed (DXY +0.84% to 100.38) and gold gave back -1.7% to 4,256.93. The split with Europe/Asia is a timing artifact — those cash markets closed before the US fade, leaving Euro Stoxx (+0.68%) and Nikkei (+0.72%) green. Net tone: orderly de-risking, not panic — credit never flinched (IG/HY OAS essentially unchanged at historic tights) — but complacency is being repriced against a Fed that has taken cuts off the table.
