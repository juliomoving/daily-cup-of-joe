🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 58/100 — Two inflations, moving in opposite directions — and the market is only pricing one of them. The slow-moving, wage-and-services core is genuinely cooling: Core CPI is 2.6% YoY, Median CPI 2.71455%, the 16% Trimmed-Mean CPI 2.62829%, Median PCE 2.66027% and Trimmed-Mean PCE 2.23%, with the computed direction on Median PCE, Trimmed-Mean PCE, Sticky CPI (2.8427%) and Core Sticky CPI (2.8069%) all reading falling. The Employment Cost Index at 0.9% YoY and average hourly earnings at 3.2% remove the wage-spiral argument entirely — a labor panel showing -23.0k payrolls and 7,359k openings does not generate wage inflation. But the fast-moving side is reheating: Flexible CPI is running 5.0616% over twelve months and is the one measure the computed panel flags as rising, sitting at the 77th percentile of its history with a z-score of 0.56. That is energy, food and goods — and Brent up 1.29% today with a live Hormuz risk is exactly the channel that keeps it elevated. Breadth backs the split verdict: 8.1% of the PCE basket is still rising faster than 10% and another 15.4% between 5% and 10%, so roughly a quarter of the basket is running hot, while 24.3% is in the 0-2% zone and 28.1% is outright falling. That is a basket pulling apart, not converging. The gap that should worry everyone is Core PCE at 3.28653% YoY against a 5-year breakeven of only 2.2299% and a 1-year breakeven of 1.6873% — the bond market has priced inflation back to target within a year while the actual core measure sits more than a point above it. Verdict: disinflation in the sticky core is real but slower than markets assume, the goods-and-energy side is re-accelerating, and the 1-year breakeven at 1.6873% is the most mispriced number on this page. Persistence is moderate and two-sided, not benign.
