IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The useful way to hold this is to separate what is expensive from what is fragile. What is expensive: aggregate index-level valuation. CAPE around 40.5x (analyst estimate) and market cap to GDP around 215% (analyst estimate) are both at or beyond anything in US history including 2000, and top-10 concentration near 41.5% (analyst estimate) means the S&P 500 is functionally a levered bet on a dozen balance sheets. But — and this is the crucial distinction from 1999 — those companies earn money. A 37.56x Nasdaq trailing P/E is only 14% of the 175x of March 2000 precisely because there are real profits in the denominator now. The 2000 Nasdaq was priced on eyeballs; this one is priced on cash flow, expensively. What is fragile: the price of risk everywhere else. IG spreads at 0.80% (133% of the 2000 condition), Baa-10Y at 160bp (151%), the NFCI at -0.56 (163%), VIX at 14.2 (200%), and the commodity-equity ratio at 0.02 (200%) form a coherent picture — every mechanism that normally cushions an equity decline is currently priced at or beyond its most complacent historical setting. That is what makes a modest valuation shock capable of producing an immodest outcome. The drawdown reference is sobering and worth stating precisely: from 5,048.62 on 10 March 2000 to 1,114.11 on 9 October 2002, the Nasdaq fell 78.0% over 31 months. Nobody thinks that is the base case here. But the anchored charts are instructive about where we are in the cycle rather than where the top is. Anchored at the October-2022 low, the current run is 48 months in and stands at 241.9 — a 2.4x. The dot-com analogue anchored at the October-1990 bear bottom did not peak until month 113, at 1,423.9 — a 14x. Anchored at the 1994 pre-internet reset low, it peaked at month 69 at 665.3 — a 6.7x. On both analogues, today's advance is a fraction of what the 1990s produced in both magnitude and duration, and the computed valuation marker sits at just 21% of the peak condition. If you are pattern-matching, this looks like 1996-97 rather than 1999-2000: extended, richly valued, but nowhere near the terminal blow-off. The signposts that would move me are specific and mostly not flashing. Consumer confidence at 89.4 (27% of peak) tells you retail is not euphoric — bubbles top with the public all-in, and the public is not. AAII net bullishness at 2.1pp (55%) is close to neutral. ISM Manufacturing at 54.6 and Services at 55.4 (8% and 6% of the rollover condition) say the economy is expanding, and every dot-com-scale unwind in history was accompanied by growth rolling over first. What I would watch: margin debt already growing at 45.6% year over year (63% of the 2000 peak) is the leverage building underneath; SOX at 51.67x (79%) is the leadership group getting genuinely expensive; SLOOS at 0.0% net tightening means banks have not yet turned, so the first positive print is the signal, not the confirmation; and HY at 3.00% in the computed panel is the cushion that must widen before anything breaks. The practical conclusion: do not sell this market because it is expensive — expensive markets stay expensive for years, and the 1990s analogue says there could be a great deal of runway left. Do refuse to be paid the 3rd percentile of history for credit risk, do own volatility while it costs 14, and do recognise that concentration near 41.5% means index diversification is now largely an illusion. The bubble question and the fragility question have different answers, and only the second one is urgent.
