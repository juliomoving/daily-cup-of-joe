Bubble-analog — today's Nasdaq vs the dot-com cycle (two anchors)

WHY TWO START DATES?
Each line is rebased to 100 at a low the tech bull launched from, so elapsed time lines up. Oct-2022 is today's anchor — it was the prior bear-market bottom. Oct-1990 is the dot-com cycle's equivalent (its literal prior bear bottom). But 1990 predates the internet mania by ~4 years, so the second chart re-anchors the dot-com line to the 1994 reset low, just before the surge ignited, for a tighter like-for-like. Today sits very early under the strict 1990 analog, further along under the 1994 surge analog.

IF THIS RHYMES WITH 2000
Nasdaq fell -78.0% peak→trough (2000-03-10 → 2002-10-09, ~31 months); it took ~15 years to reclaim the high.

The honest read is 'concentration risk dressed as a bull market.' The melt-up is real and supported by genuine AI earnings (Micron's HBM sold out through 2026), but it is dangerously narrow — Info Tech +21% YTD carrying the tape while half the S&P sectors are flat-to-negative on the year, top-10 weight near 40%, and semis valued past their dot-com analog. The protective factor vs 2000 is that aggregate index P/E and IPO speculation are far tamer; the offsetting danger is that credit and volatility complacency are MORE extreme than 2000, so the cushion if the AI narrative wobbles is thinner. This is a market to ride with trailing stops, not to chase at the 100th percentile.
