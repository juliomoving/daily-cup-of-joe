🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 61/100 — Sticky, not accelerating — and the headline core numbers disagree with each other in a way that matters. Core PCE at 3.29% year-over-year is the single most uncomfortable print in this panel and sits well above the Fed's target, while Core CPI at 2.60% tells a materially softer story. The computed Core-Trend Sub-Score (rendered separately, tagged 'computed') resolves that argument toward the middle: the median and trimmed-mean measures cluster between 2.23% and 2.84% and all four of the PCE/trimmed measures are flat-to-falling in direction. The genuine warning is composition, not level. Flexible CPI is running at 5.06% and is the only measure the computed panel marks as rising, with a percentile rank of 77 and a z-score of 0.56 — the volatile, energy-and-goods-sensitive half of the basket is reaccelerating exactly as Brent rallies 1.70%. Breadth from the Dallas Fed distribution is genuinely two-handed: 29.3% of the PCE basket is rising faster than 3% (with 8.1% rising above 10% and another 15.4% between 5% and 10%), but 52.4% is either below 2% or outright falling. That is not a broad inflation, it is a concentrated one — and concentrated inflations are the kind that a central bank can wait out unless energy re-broadens them. Markets agree and then some: the 5-year breakeven at 2.2047% and the 1-year at 1.5979% show investors pricing near-term disinflation, an inversion of breakevens that is essentially a bet the flexible-CPI impulse is transitory. Labor supports patience — payrolls at 57k, openings at 7,359k, quits at 2.2% and the ECI at 0.9% all point to a cooling wage channel, with the 4.2% unemployment rate and 199k claims saying cooling, not cracking. Verdict: inflation persists near 2.5-3%, does not spiral, and does not fall fast enough to hand Warsh a cut. Watch the crude pass-through into flexible CPI — that is the one path that changes this read within a quarter.
