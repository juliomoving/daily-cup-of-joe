The dominant story into the close was oil: a tenth consecutive night of US strikes on Iran sent Brent up +4.37% to 94.99 and WTI +2.95% to 86.83, and equity leadership rotated hard into what benefits from higher energy and lower rates sensitivity. The S&P 500 slipped -0.14% to 7,498.96, the Dow was flat, but the growth-heavy Nasdaq fell -0.57% and small-cap Russell 2000 dropped -0.92%, the day's worst — a classic 'war-tax-on-growth' tape. Yet the striking thing is complacency: VIX eased to 16.64, credit risk premiums sit in the 2nd–4th percentile of history, and Europe rallied (FTSE +1.24%, energy-led). Treasury yields backed up (10Y +2.6bp to 4.65%) as sticky inflation and an openly hawkish new Fed chair, Kevin Warsh, keep cuts off the table ahead of next Wednesday's FOMC. Versus the prior session the mood shifted from broad risk-on toward defensive-plus-energy; sentiment is best described as uneasy calm — the war is escalating while volatility and spreads act as if nothing is wrong.

The frothy pieces are valuation and complacency: mega-cap concentration (an estimated ~39% top-10 weight, analyst estimate), an elevated Shiller CAPE near 39x (analyst estimate) and a market-cap-to-GDP 'Buffett indicator' around 208% (analyst estimate) all sit in historically expensive territory, and long-term EPS-growth expectations are elevated (~74/100, analyst estimate) — a BofA bear-market signpost. The offset is that price-stretch and euphoria remain moderate — the Nasdaq is only ~7% above its 200-day average and leadership is actually narrowing (Info Tech -2.7% on the month). Net: a stretched, complacent, late-cycle market vulnerable to an external shock (war, oil, a hawkish Fed) rather than a market on the literal edge of a 2000-style top.

⚔️Iran War

The conflict is escalating, not de-escalating. As of Jul 20–21, US forces completed a tenth consecutive night of strikes on Iranian command centers, missile/drone launch sites, maritime capabilities and air defenses, with reported explosions from the Hormuz littoral to Shiraz and Isfahan. The death toll from the latest US strikes has risen to around 50, and the Pentagon reported three US service members killed over the weekend (two in Jordan, one in northern Iraq). Diplomatically, Tehran has suspended its commitments under the June 14 memorandum of understanding (which had aimed to end the conflict within 60 days) after the fighting resumed over Iran striking commercial vessels that bypassed its preapproved shipping route. The US is surging additional F-16/F-35 aircraft and tankers to the region, and Kuwait reported damage to a 'vital' oil-sector site. This is the direct driver of today's oil spike (Brent +4.37%), with Strait of Hormuz disruption the key tail risk markets are underpricing.
