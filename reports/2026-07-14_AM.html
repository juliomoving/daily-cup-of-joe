🔥Is Inflation Persistent?

Persistence score: 71/100 — Sticky and about to get an energy tailwind — the wrong combination. The core-trend measures cluster in the high-60s-to-high-70s percentiles of their own history (Core Sticky CPI at 3.09% and Sticky CPI at 3.12%, both rising; 16% trimmed-mean CPI at 2.91%, rising; median PCE at 2.82%, flat), which says the persistent, slow-moving component of inflation has stopped falling and is drifting the wrong way. That is not the profile of a disinflation that is nearly done. Against that, market-implied expectations are strikingly relaxed: the 5-year breakeven sits at 2.28% and the 1-year at just 1.08%, and Core CPI at 2.60% flatters the picture. Wages are cooperating (average hourly earnings 3.5%, ECI 0.9%), so this is not a wage-price spiral. It is a services-and-shelter stickiness problem that is now being handed a fresh energy shock — Flexible CPI is already at 6.99% (85th percentile, rising), and Brent is up 3.77% today. Verdict: the last mile is not being walked, and Hormuz just made the hill steeper. A Fed that was hoping to cut is going to be arguing about hiking.
